01 / The people

Saved everywhere.
Visited nowhere.

NYC students discover places on Instagram, through friends, and on Google Maps. But saving a location is easy; turning it into an actual afternoon out is harder. I spoke with three students about their most recent city outings.

01 / BEATRICE · UNDERGRADUATE
“I just never really look at them again.”
On places saved in Google Maps. Her East Village outing was spontaneous, and deciding where to eat took too long.
02 / VANCE · MASTER'S
“It's hard to know how many stops you can realistically fit into one afternoon.”
On exploring Roosevelt Island. Distance and time make otherwise interesting saved places difficult to visit.
03 / JOSH · MASTER'S
“I probably have like 30 restaurant screenshots on my phone.”
On collecting NYC recommendations. His Astoria group spent roughly 20 minutes deciding where to eat.
The problem isn't a lack of recommendations. It's turning inspiration into a plan.Full interview log ↗
02 / The idea

From “one day”
to today.

ELSEWHERE connects the places you want to go with the time you actually have. Start with a sentence, refine your preferences, then take a route grounded in real NYC locations—not AI-invented businesses.

01 / CREATE

Plan an
afternoon.

“Three hours. Bookstores, coffee, and something unexpected.” Describe a mood or choose time, budget, neighborhood, and how you want to get around. Get a plausible route you can edit.

AI-assisted itinerary · Verified places
02 / COLLECT

Make the city
your own.

Save interesting spots and journeys in My NYC Atlas. Bring saved places back into new plans, rather than leaving them buried in screenshots and bookmarks.

Personal map · Saved places & routes
03 / The focus

Deep on design.
Useful by default.

My focus is Design: an interface people understand without instruction, where expressive editorial visual language and precise map interactions make discovery feel personal.

DES
IGN.

NYC editorial × modern atlas × playful interaction.

I want to go deep on visual identity, flows, accessible contrast, responsive maps, clear states, and subtle motion that helps people act. AI supports the experience; the design is what I will refine most.

04 / The roadmap

A small first trip.
A bigger world.

Start with an end-to-end NYC experience; learn from real use before adding complexity.

NOV 10
Start

Validate the location data source and AI costs; refine the user flow and design prototypes. Build and test the smallest trustworthy route-planning path first.

NOV 17
V1 · Live

Users can sign in, describe an outing, get a route based on real NYC places, adjust the plan, and save places or routes in My NYC Atlas. Deploy a usable site.

NOV 24
Feedback

No class. Test the live experience with students, watch where they hesitate, and refine the highest-friction interactions.

DEC 01
V2 · Fair

Polish the visual system and map interactions; add a lightweight NYC Journal for photos and memories if the core experience is solid. Present a tested, cohesive product.

FUTURE
Beyond NYC

NYC-first, not NYC-only. Once the New York experience is proven, explore expansion to cities such as Chicago, Boston, and San Francisco. Multi-city support is a long-term vision, not part of the November 17 V1 or December 1 V2 scope.

05 / The tools

Technology in
service of the idea.

A practical stack for a real, end-to-end product. Keep mapping and location data grounded in actual sources, and choose AI and API usage that can stay within student/free-tier budgets.

Next.js + TypeScript

Product interface and typed application logic.

Tailwind CSS

Responsive design system and detailed UI refinement.

MapLibre GL JS

Interactive maps and journey visualization.

Supabase

Saved locations, routes, and row-level access controls.

Clerk

Sign-in and personal collections.

AI model API · TBD

Understand preferences and compose routes from verified locations.

Real place data · TBD

OpenStreetMap/open datasets and curated spots, pending coverage and terms checks.

Claude Desktop + MCP/CLI

Supabase MCP, Clerk CLI, Git/GitHub, and Vercel CLI for iterative development and deployment.

Agent workflow: Claude Desktop is the development agent; project-scoped Supabase MCP assists with database access, Clerk CLI supports authentication setup, and Git/GitHub plus Vercel CLI support versioning and deployment. These are planned tools for the final project, not claims that ELSEWHERE is already built. Read the scoping interview ↗

Cost & accuracy plan: Check each provider's free-tier terms and usage limits before implementation. Use verified place records; calculate travel distances or times from appropriate routing data; label uncertainty. Do not invent opening hours or recommendations and present them as facts. Defer social import, weather-aware rerouting, and group collaboration until after V1.
