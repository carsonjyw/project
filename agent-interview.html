Agent Interview Log.

How the final-project idea was narrowed, questioned, and scoped before the proposal was written.

Documentation note: This is a concise, chronological reconstruction of our actual one-question-at-a-time project-scoping discussion. It summarizes decisions rather than pretending to be a verbatim chat transcript. The three separate human interview transcripts are reproduced on the User Interview Log page.
QUESTION / DECISION 01

Project direction

We compared ideas for the final project. I selected ELSEWHERE, an AI-assisted city discovery product, and chose New York City as the first city.

QUESTION / DECISION 02

Who is it for?

I identified students and young adults who live in or regularly explore NYC as the initial audience. I said I could reach three student interviewees.

QUESTION / DECISION 03

How do they discover places?

I identified social platforms and map apps as likely starting points. This was a hypothesis to check in real interviews, not a reported research finding.

QUESTION / DECISION 04

What problem should it address?

I selected all four proposed pain points as working hypotheses: information overload, scattered saves, difficulty grouping places into feasible routes, and generic recommendations.

QUESTION / DECISION 05

What belongs in v1?

I chose B + C: AI-assisted city routes plus a saved-places personal atlas. Sign-in and a working end-to-end flow are part of the first release.

QUESTION / DECISION 06

What waits until later?

I chose to defer social-media import, weather-aware rerouting, collaboration, and a full journal until after V1. Those are possibilities, not V2 commitments.

QUESTION / DECISION 07

How should people interact?

I selected both natural-language prompts and structured controls for time, neighborhood, budget, interests, and travel preferences.

QUESTION / DECISION 08

Where do places come from?

I selected a combination of verified location datasets and a curated collection. We agreed that the AI must not fabricate businesses or unsupported opening hours.

QUESTION / DECISION 09

What is the creative direction?

I chose NYC Editorial × Modern Atlas × Experimental Creative. The central project focus is Design, rather than simply demonstrating an AI API.

QUESTION / DECISION 10

What should v2 emphasize?

I chose a lightweight exploration journal and deeper design refinement as the preferred December 1 direction.

QUESTION / DECISION 11

What makes a good itinerary?

I chose a balance of efficient, realistic routing and surprising discoveries relevant to the rider's interests.

QUESTION / DECISION 12

Reality check: three interviews

Beatrice, Vance, and Josh described forgotten saves, practical travel constraints, and decision friction. Those real answers support the core v1 direction; the full transcripts are on the adjacent page.

Scope decision: V1 on November 17 is a deployable NYC itinerary planner and personal atlas. December 1 focuses on visual polish and a journal if the core user journey is stable. Planned services and API costs will be checked before implementation.
