User Interview Log.

Three conversations about how students actually discover, save, and visit places in New York City.

These are the three student interviews provided for the proposal. Answers are reproduced verbatim. They are qualitative observations, not a representative survey.
INTERVIEW 01 / Undergraduate student

Beatrice

Q1. Tell me about the last time you explored somewhere new in NYC. How did you decide where to go?

I went to the East Village last weekend. I hadn't really spent much time there before. My friend knew a few places around that area, so we just decided to go. We didn't really have a plan. We just walked around, checked out some stores, and got food.

Q2. What apps or websites did you use to find places?

Mostly Google Maps. Sometimes Instagram if someone posts a place that looks interesting. But I don't really search for places that much unless I'm already going somewhere.

Q3. Have you ever saved a place you wanted to visit but never went? What happened?

Yeah, I have a bunch of places saved on Google Maps. I just never really look at them again. Usually when I go out, I end up going wherever my friends want to go, so I kind of forget about my own list.

Q4. How did you decide which places to visit together and in what order?

We didn't really decide beforehand. We went to one store, then saw something else nearby and walked over. If something was too far, we just skipped it.

Q5. What was the most frustrating part of planning that outing?

Probably figuring out where to eat. We kept looking at different places, but either the reviews weren't great or the wait was too long. We spent way too much time just deciding on food.

INTERVIEW 02 / Master's student

Vance

Q1. Tell me about the last time you explored somewhere new in NYC. How did you decide where to go?

I went to Roosevelt Island a few weeks ago. I'd heard about the tram, but I'd never taken it before. I had some free time that weekend, so I figured I'd check it out. I looked up a few things to do there before going.

Q2. What apps or websites did you use to find places?

Mainly Google Maps. I usually check reviews, photos, and opening hours. Sometimes I use Reddit too, especially if I want to know whether a place is actually worth visiting.

Q3. Have you ever saved a place you wanted to visit but never went? What happened?

Yeah, quite a few. I think the main issue is location. I'll find something interesting and save it, but then I realize it's like 40 minutes away from where I usually spend my time. Unless I have another reason to go to that area, I probably won't make a separate trip.

Q4. How did you decide which places to visit together and in what order?

I usually check the map first and see what's close to what. For Roosevelt Island, I planned to take the tram, walk around the southern part of the island, and then explore a bit more before heading back. I try to avoid going back and forth.

Q5. What was the most frustrating part of planning that outing?

I guess figuring out how much time everything would take. Google Maps tells you the walking distance, but it doesn't really tell you how long you might want to spend at each place. So it's hard to know how many stops you can realistically fit into one afternoon.

INTERVIEW 03 / Master's student

Josh

Q1. Tell me about the last time you explored somewhere new in NYC. How did you decide where to go?

I went to Astoria with some friends last month. One of them said there were some really good food spots there, and I'd never been, so we were like, okay, let's go. That was pretty much it.

Q2. What apps or websites did you use to find places?

Google Maps, mostly. I also get recommendations from friends. Sometimes I'll see a restaurant on Instagram and just send it to our group chat.

Q3. Have you ever saved a place you wanted to visit but never went? What happened?

Oh, definitely. I screenshot stuff all the time and then completely forget about it. I probably have like 30 restaurant screenshots on my phone. Half of them I don't even remember where they are.

Q4. How did you decide which places to visit together and in what order?

Honestly, we didn't. We picked a restaurant first, and then after eating we just looked at what was nearby. Someone suggested going to a park, but it was kind of far, so we ended up getting coffee instead.

Q5. What was the most frustrating part of planning that outing?

Probably the group chat. Everyone was sending different places, and nobody could agree on anything. We spent like 20 minutes arguing about where to eat. By the time we decided, we were already pretty hungry.

What I learned: saved places are easily forgotten; distance and available time shape what is feasible; coordinating an outing can be frustrating. These observations informed the V1 focus on practical route planning plus a personal saved-places map.
